# Engineering Ladder

Owner: Helen Vasquez, VP of Engineering. Version 2025.1, published January 8, 2025.

The engineering ladder describes what we expect from engineers at each level and how promotions work. It is a guide for growth conversations, not a checklist: people are promoted for consistently operating at the next level, not for completing a list of tasks.

## Levels

Lumen Labs has two parallel tracks. The individual contributor track has six levels:

- E1: Associate Engineer
- E2: Software Engineer
- E3: Senior Software Engineer
- E4: Staff Engineer
- E5: Senior Staff Engineer
- E6: Principal Engineer

The management track has three levels: M1 Engineering Manager, M2 Senior Engineering Manager, and M3 Director of Engineering. An E4 and an M1 are considered equivalent in scope, and engineers can move between the tracks at that point without a change in pay.

## Expectations by level

### E1 and E2

An E1 completes well-defined tasks with guidance from a more senior engineer. An E2 delivers features independently within a single service and is expected to take part in the on-call rotation once they have finished onboarding.

### E3

An E3 leads multi-week projects within their team, reviews other people's designs, and mentors E1 and E2 engineers. Reaching E3 is also what makes an engineer eligible to train as an incident commander.

### E4

An E4 leads at least one cross-team project lasting one quarter or longer, writes design documents that are reviewed by more than one team, and sets technical direction for a domain such as ingestion or ranking. E4 engineers are expected to improve the systems and processes around them, for example by driving postmortem action items to completion.

### E5 and E6

An E5 shapes technical direction across the whole engineering organization and is trusted to make decisions that affect several teams at once. An E6 works directly with the CTO on company-wide strategy and represents Lumen Labs technically to customers and the wider industry. Lumen Labs expects to have only a handful of E6 engineers at any time.

## Promotion process

Promotion cycles happen twice a year, in April and October. For each candidate, the manager writes a promotion packet that includes peer feedback from at least 3 colleagues, at least one of whom must work on a different team.

Packets are reviewed by a calibration committee chaired by the VP of Engineering. The committee includes every manager at M2 or above and every individual contributor at E5 or above. The committee's goal is to keep the bar consistent across teams, so it may ask for more evidence before deciding.

Engineers must spend at least 12 months at their current level before being promoted to E4 or any higher level. There is no minimum time at level for promotions up to E3. Approved promotions take effect on the first day of the month after the cycle closes.

## Compensation

Every level has a salary band, and the bands are reviewed each January against market data. A promotion always comes with a base salary increase of at least 8%. Individual salaries and the exact band ranges are confidential and are shared only between an employee and their manager.

## Performance reviews

Formal performance reviews happen once a year in March, just before the April promotion cycle. Each review combines a self-review, a manager review, and feedback from at least two peers. Ratings use a four-point scale: needs improvement, solid, strong, and exceptional.

## Growth plans

Every engineer should have a written growth plan that names one or two skills to develop over the next six months. Managers revisit growth plans in their regular one-on-ones at least once a month. The annual learning and development stipend can be used for courses or books that support the plan.
